# Feedback on the dissertation draft — 23 September 2026

Read it end to end. It is a strong thesis, and Chapter 5 is the best part: the limitations
section is more rigorous than most published papers in this area. You state that each
configuration was trained once, that the intervals cover episodes and not training runs,
that a resolved difference is not an attributed one, and that one of your own adversaries
*helped* its victim. That restraint is what makes the negative result credible, and it is
the standard the rest of the project is now being held to.

What follows is ordered by how much it matters, not by where it appears in the document.

## 1. Something new that affects your Chapter 4

We repeated the FGSM measurement on **three independently trained victims** per
architecture, for the four non-GNN variants:

| Variant | canonical | s1042 | s2042 | mean | sd |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CC-Simple | +0.37 | +2.70 | **+5.11** | +2.72 | 2.37 |
| CC-Duelling | +2.53 | +2.57 | +5.81 | +3.63 | 1.88 |
| LC-Simple | +3.27 | +3.49 | +2.00 | +2.92 | 0.81 |
| LC-Duelling | +1.24 | +1.19 | +4.66 | +2.36 | 1.99 |

The between-variant ordering does not replicate. Every mean ±1 sd band overlaps every
other, and the spread between variant means (1.3 pp) is smaller than the spread between
seeds of one variant (0.8–2.4 pp).

This matters for you in two places. Your **Table 4.5** reports the FGSM baseline per
variant — those are canonical-victim numbers, and the CC-Simple row in particular is not
representative. More importantly, **RQ3 asks which victim architectures are most
vulnerable**, and the honest answer is now that the evidence does not support ranking them.

You already apply exactly this caution to your *adversaries* in §5.2.1 ("the pattern may
reflect which adversaries happened to train well as much as any property of the
architectures"). The same argument applies to the *victims*, and you can say so in one
paragraph, citing this replication. That strengthens §5.1.4 rather than weakening it —
you predicted the failure mode and it turned out to be real.

The three GNN variants are being retrained under two extra seeds now; I will send the
numbers when they finish.

## 2. Two things worth fixing in the argument

**The random control is weaker than FGSM's — and that works in your favour.** You spot this
in §5.2.3: yours draws each feature uniformly from [−ε, ε] (expected magnitude ε/2) while
the FGSM study drew ±ε at full magnitude. A weaker control makes your own gaps *larger*. So
your attack is flattered by the comparison and *still* does not beat the baseline, which
makes the negative result **conservative**. Say that explicitly — at the moment it reads as
a flaw when it is actually support. (You correctly avoid the real trap in §4.3 by comparing
attack arms directly rather than differencing the two gaps, but Tables 4.1–4.3 and Table 4.5
sit pages apart with gaps measured against different references, and only one paragraph
warns. Put the warning in the table captions.)

**Anchor the effect sizes to the damage ceiling.** You report gaps of 0.3–5.9 pp "on a clean
delivery ratio of about 77%". The inherited work established roughly **21 pp of available
damage** at this operating point. Saying *"the learned adversary extracted at most ~28% of
the damage available to an ideal observation attacker"* is far more informative than
"+5.90 pp", and it ties your H1 conclusion directly to the framework you inherited. The
ceiling data already exists — this is cheap and it would noticeably strengthen Chapter 5.

## 3. An internal tension a jury will find

§3.3.2 argues the duelling head is degenerate — "an extra parallel projection rather than a
value–advantage separation". §4.4.1 then states "the duelling value head **is** the
vulnerable axis". §5.1.4 partly walks it back. The §4.4.1 phrasing is stronger than §5.1.4
permits.

Both can be true, and the resolving sentence is short: same function class, but a different
training trajectory produces different weights, so any vulnerability difference is a
property of the *learned solution*, not of the head's expressive power. Add it where you
make the §4.4.1 claim. You set this trap yourself in §3.3.2, so you will certainly be asked.

Minor, same family: your single headline positive (+2.21 pp on LC-Simple, the one
configuration that beats FGSM) comes from the **ungated** setting — the one you twice flag
as off-distribution, since the adversaries were trained with the gate at b = 0.25.
Acknowledge that at the sentence in §5.1.1, not only in limitations.

## 5. Two things you got right that the other thesis did not

Worth knowing, because it tells you where your description is the authoritative one:

- **The 94-dimension truncation.** Your footnote on p.19 is correct: the environment
  assembles a longer vector and truncates, dropping the last two path utilisations and the
  hops-remaining feature. Gonçalo's Eq. 4.3 still computes 96.
- **The degenerate duelling head.** You derived it independently in §3.3.2.

Also, your "FGSM-parity vs full clamp" distinction rests on the observation that the
baseline clamps only the first four features. That is exactly right, and Gonçalo is
correcting his Chapter 6 to describe it properly — so the premise of your Chapter 4 will be
consistent with his method section rather than contradicting it.
